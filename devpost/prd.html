acu-primer

A course author turns a textbook section into short lessons that adapt to the wrong answer a student gives, keep working offline, and show whether a person has checked them.

Scope said "a student gets a check wrong, and the lesson changes course." Now we know what that means: the fallback depends on which wrong answer, unplanned answers get a general fallback, and a failed fallback sends the student back to the material with no third level.

The core journey

One OpenIntro section (5.1) all the way around the loop. Click a step, or use the buttons.

What a wrong answer does

The student givesThey get
A wrong answer the tool anticipatedThe fallback for that answer's misconception: a targeted re-explanation, a simpler question, or both (the author's choice)
A wrong answer nobody planned for"That isn't quite right." Then a simpler question to try first, if there is one. Otherwise, re-read or think it through and try again, with a pointer to the simpler explanation if one exists.
A wrong answer to the fallback questionNo third level. A suggestion to go back through the material, and they can carry on.

The look

Simple, uncluttered and smooth. Picked at review: Clean study for light mode and Night desk for dark mode, following the device setting. Quiet paper stays here for comparison. Try the wrong answer in each.

Screens, behaviors and checks

Each opens to what it does and how we'll know it works.

States and edge cases

First visitName entry, then an empty overview. Continue opens module 1.
Returning, same deviceProgress is there.
Offline after loadingEverything works, and events wait in the browser.
Never loaded, no connectionThe course can't open. It has to load once online.
Unreviewed lessonsHidden from students by default. If the owner switches them on, they carry an obvious "not yet reviewed" mark.
Empty courseStudents see a note that nothing is ready yet.
No students yetThe per-student view says so.
Who can do whatAnyone with the link plays signed-off lessons. Only the owner gets past the gate.

Confirmed at review

  • A name identifies a student on one device. Same name on one device shares progress, and progress doesn't move between devices.
  • Unreviewed lessons are hidden completely by default, not shown as locked. The author can switch on showing them.

Now, later, out

What we're building now
  • Name entry, course overview with progress, Continue
  • Short modules with checks from the first supported list
  • Fallbacks per wrong answer, the general fallback, the two-level stop
  • Offline play after first load, with events sent later
  • Authoring session for one section, with author choice over checks and fallbacks
  • Lesson review with every fallback, sign-off, and the owner's switch
  • Per-student log
Deferred from the proof of concept deferred
  • AI read-out of a student ("what we can tell"): model calls per student, and it needs teacher research first
  • Research into what a teacher needs from the per-student view
  • An online AI tutor in the player, and paid and free versions
  • Check types beyond the first list, added when a course needs them. Full list (details in curriculum-edtech/content-graph.md):
    • Any content: match, put in order, fill in the blanks, click the right spot, build something, free text, a "why?" and "how sure?" layer
    • Algorithms and code: Parsons problems, find the bug, step through a simulation, recursion tree, give the running time, write code
    • Maths: type an expression, derive line by line, arrange proof blocks, fill in a missing step, find the error, classify the master-theorem case
    • Stats: sketch a distribution, estimate on a number line, generated practice
    • Conceptual: answer plus "because" plus confidence, a question before the reading, reflect, spaced review, scenarios
  • Progress across devices (needs accounts)
