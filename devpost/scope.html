acu-primer

A course-authoring tool and the lesson player that runs what it makes. Lessons adapt when a student gets something wrong, and every lesson shows whether a person has checked it.

The kernel: one lesson goes all the way around a loop

Asking a chatbot for a quiz gets you step 1 at best. acu-primer is the whole loop, with a person verifying the content before students see it.

Who it's for

The course author

A university lecturer, or whoever builds the course, with a textbook and a need for interactive lessons they trust. They never see the internal format. For Oct 9 that's Isaac, dogfooding. After that come a lecturer friend and the CS department HOD.

Open: what authors use today. 3-prd can ask if it matters.

The student

Opens a course link and types their name. Works through small modules with activities, and gets help when stuck instead of a red X.

What a wrong answer does

Click through a student's path through one check.

What "working" looks like

The app opens at a link. In a demo under three minutes, on one OpenIntro Statistics section (Creative Commons, so it can be shown publicly):

  1. The author uploads the section and works through it with the tool. It drafts a lesson with checks and fallbacks, and the author accepts, changes or drops them.
  2. The author signs off the lesson. The review marks are obvious.
  3. A student opens the link, types a name, and gets a check wrong. They get the re-explanation or the simpler quiz, fail that too, and the lesson points them back to the material.
  4. The author opens the per-student view and sees what that student did.

The "oh, that's cool" beat isn't picked yet. It could be the lesson changing course, a page becoming a lesson in front of you, or the author and tool working it out together. The loop demo shows all three, so the video can choose later.

What's in, what waits, what's out

Proof of concept now
  • Player: small modules, checks, up to two fallbacks per check, and the "go back through the material" stop
  • Event logging and a basic per-student view
  • Authoring session for one section, with fallbacks the author chooses
  • One sign-off per lesson, with review marks the player respects (unreviewed lessons hidden by default, and the owner can change that)
  • Student = a name typed in at the link. Author side behind a simple gate.
Also in the Oct 9 build if time allows, cut from the bottom
  • CLRS chapter 4 course: six lessons, with 4.1 and 4.3 written by hand as the baseline and the rest written by the tool. The PDF never goes in the repo.
  • Lecturer-facing recommendations and automatic adaptation, demoed on simulated students
Later later

  • Smarter adaptation, informed by research on adaptive learning

Explicitly cut cut
  • Real accounts: one course owner. A typed name is enough for students.
  • Fallbacks on fallbacks: two levels at most, then back to the material, so a stuck student doesn't go around in circles.

Inspiration

  • Udacity's modules: short units with activities mixed in
  • repovive's math roadmap: concept, rules with examples, scattered quizzes, problems with hints, then takeaways, and progress shown per module ("0/41")
  • The Primer in The Diamond Age, the book that teaches its reader
