Making acu-primer look and feel less bland

Every screen, in both looks, at desktop and phone width. A ranked list of what gets in the way, what other learning and review tools do, and five proposals you can build in a few hours each. The mocks are real HTML in the app's own fonts, so you can click around them.

The short version: the player is calm and readable already. Most of the blandness comes from the author side and the home page, where nearly everything is underlined text and status labels look like buttons.

Top recommendations

In build order. The first one makes every later one cheaper.

  1. Build a component vocabulary: three button levels with hover, focus and pressed states, a badge that can't be pressed, Lucide icons, and a link colour.
  2. Rebuild the author home with it: a "Draft" badge plus a primary Review button per lesson, a meta line under each title, the brief shown as a line, a real switch, no phone overflow.
  3. Replace the review step with interactive review mode: play the module as a student inside a violet frame, guided through every path, then approve or suggest.
  4. Add New course, with eight learner presets that fill an editable brief.
  5. Give / an identity: the mark and wordmark, one plain promise, course cards, and Continue for a returning student.
  6. Pair right and wrong with icons and a little motion: a short shake on wrong, a tick that draws on right.
  7. Fix disabled buttons (grey with a reason, not dim amber) and the native file picker on the draft screen.
  8. Choose a visual direction and apply its colours last.

Brief presets

Picked from the fields other course tools ask about: level, background, prior experience, reading level, goal and time per sitting. Each fills an editable brief.

First-year university studentTaking this for credit. Comfortable with school algebra, new to this subject's notation. Worked examples, every symbol defined the first time, checks that look like exam questions.
High-school studentAged 15 to 18. Short readings, everyday examples (sport, phones, school life), no calculus. Define every new word. Short sentences, encouraging tone.
Adult self-learnerSpare time, 15 to 20 minute sittings, rusty on maths. The idea and a real example before any formula; a one-line recap at the start of each lesson.
Learning for workNeeds it for the job soon. Skip proofs and history. Workplace examples, and end each lesson with one way to use it at work.
Exam crammerExam in one or two weeks, has seen it once. Exam-style checks and common traps. Short readings; restate key formulas often.
Hands-on technical learnerA software engineer learning alone. Commands to run, code to read, numbers to compute. Precise language welcome; skip motivational framing.
Returning learnerBack after years away. Refresh each prerequisite before using it. Small steps, slow pace, a check after almost every idea.
Future teacher or tutorWill teach this. Name misconceptions and where they come from, point to the catalogue entry, suggest how to explain each idea.

What other tools do

Only what transfers to acu-primer. Every link loaded or appeared in search results; more detail and links in research.md.

Learning products

  • Brilliant: try first, then the explanation answers your attempt.
  • Duolingo: a coloured verdict banner holding Continue; big celebrations only at the end.
  • Khan Academy: kids hit Hint when they meant Check, so keep secondary actions quieter and apart.
  • Udacity: 2 to 3 minute pieces, a quiz after each, lesson cards with progress.
  • repovive: a done/total count per section and node titles that say their type.

Review tools

  • GitHub PR review: "Viewed" per file folds it away and moves a counter; it clears when the file changes.
  • Linear triage: one key per decision, comment optional.
  • Figma: comment pins drift with the layout, so pin to part ids.
  • Notion: resolve with a tick, filter to bring resolved back.

Preview as learner

  • Canvas Student View: a coloured border round the screen and a banner with Leave and Reset.
  • Rise 360: comment on the learner view; users complain comments are hard to place.
  • Moodle and Teachable: switch role or preview as student.

Buttons, badges, icons, motion

  • Primer Label, Atlassian Lozenge, Polaris Badge: status markers are small and never pressable.
  • Material states: hover, focus and pressed as layers over the button.
  • Lucide: ISC license, 2px stroke, @lucide/svelte for Svelte 5, bundled so offline still works.
  • web.dev: keep a still stand-in when motion carries meaning.

Decisions for Isaac

One at a time, in this order.

  1. Which direction feels most like acu-primer: A (Clean study, finished), B (Primer paper) or C (Bright steps)? Or a mix?
  2. Do you like the mark, a path with a dotted detour that rejoins? Or a book, a letter, or just the wordmark?
  3. Should light mode keep its blue accent, or move warmer (B's teal or another colour)?
  4. Is a fourth font, a serif for headings, worth it, or stay with the three we bundle?
  5. How much motion: none new, the small set (shake, tick, pill pop), or a celebration at the end of a lesson too?
  6. In review mode, should Approve wait until every path is tried, ask once if you skip some, or never ask?
  7. Should review mode replace the all-branches view, or sit beside it with a "Show every branch" toggle?
  8. Is violet right for review mode, or another colour, or no border?
  9. Suggestion buttons in the margin of each part, or only in the dock with a "which part?" picker?
  10. Do the eight presets cover your learners? What would you drop, rename or add?
  11. For New course, is "create, then draft the first lesson" enough for now, or do you want a course outline from a table of contents soon?
  12. Should / list courses publicly, or should students only get courses by link?
  13. Should the author side get its own top bar (wordmark, "Author", your courses)?
