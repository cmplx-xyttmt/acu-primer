Three visual directions

Each keeps one thing on screen at a time and the same fonts. They differ in how much warmth and play they add. Every direction uses the same component vocabulary (components.html) and the same mark: a path that takes a dotted detour and rejoins, which is what a lesson does after a wrong answer.

A. Clean study, finished

The smallest change. The current colours stay. It adds the wordmark, real buttons and cards with a soft shadow, icons, a tinted panel behind the course title, and an icon on every verdict. Bland goes away mostly because things now look clickable and grouped.

B. Primer paper

Leans on the name. The Primer in The Diamond Age is a book, so this looks like a well-made workbook: a warm paper ground, a deep teal accent in light mode, small monospace labels ("MODULE 3 OF 7"), key terms marked with a highlighter stroke, and hints styled as a dashed margin note. Night desk keeps its amber and gains the same warmth. Room for small line drawings later.

C. Bright steps

The most playful, closer to Duolingo and Brilliant. Chunky buttons with a thick bottom edge that press down, a coloured course banner with a progress ring, a row of module pills instead of a thin bar, and the verdict as a full-width banner at the bottom of the screen. A wrong answer gives the picked option one short shake (off under reduced motion).
