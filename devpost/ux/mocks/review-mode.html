5.1 Point estimates and sampling variability · Module 2 of 10

Two kinds of error

The error in an estimate is the gap between p̂ and p. It has two parts.

Sampling error is the luck of the draw: one random sample comes out a bit high, another a bit low. It shrinks as the sample size n grows.

Bias is a tendency to miss in the same direction every time, for example because of how a question is worded or who gets asked. A bigger sample doesn't fix bias. Careful data collection does.
